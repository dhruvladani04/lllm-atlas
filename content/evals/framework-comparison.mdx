Framework comparisons usually list features in a grid and let you count ticks. That grid is
wrong within a quarter, and it answers a question nobody has. The question people actually
have is: **my application has this shape — what should I reach for?**

So this page is organised by archetype, and every claim on it carries a date.

<Callout type="warning">
  Everything below was checked on the date stamped under the table. Feature sets in this
  area move faster than documentation does. If that date is more than a few months old by
  the time you read this, treat the specifics as a starting point for your own check
  rather than as current fact — the page will tell you when it has gone stale.
</Callout>

## Choosing by shape

**You are building RAG.** Start with RAGAS. Its whole design is the retriever/generator
split that the [RAG guide](/evals/rag) argues for, and you will not have to construct that
split yourself. Add Phoenix when you have enough traffic for retrieval debugging to pay off.

**You are building an agent.** You need tracing before you need metrics, because you cannot
score a trajectory you cannot see. LangSmith or Langfuse first — Langfuse if the traces
contain data you would rather self-host — then DeepEval or Inspect for the scoring described
in the [agentic guide](/evals/agentic-systems).

**You are building plain generation or a chat feature.** DeepEval in CI, pytest-style, and
promptfoo when you need to sweep prompts and models against each other rather than score one
pipeline.

**You are doing structured extraction.** You probably need less than you think: per-field
precision and recall against references, plus a schema-validity rate. promptfoo's matrices
fit this well, and a plain test suite fits it even better.

**You are red-teaming.** promptfoo for jailbreak and injection probes; Inspect if you are
evaluating models rather than an application.

**You already live in an MLOps stack.** Evidently or MLflow keep eval results beside the
rest of your experiments. Neither has the metric depth of the specialists, and that is often
an acceptable trade against having one fewer tool.

## The choice that matters more than the tool

Two decisions outrank framework selection, and both are yours regardless of what you pick:

**Where evals run.** A framework that only runs on your laptop will stop being run. Put the
offline suite in CI where a regression fails a build, and put sampling in production where
online evals can see the traffic the golden set never had.

**Who owns the golden set.** The set decides what "better" means. If nobody owns it, it
ossifies, and you spend a year optimising against questions that stopped being
representative months ago. Review it on a schedule and record when you last did — the same
discipline this page applies to itself.

<Callout type="note">
  Pick the smallest thing that measures the unit you actually care about. Most teams adopt
  a platform and use four of its metrics. Starting with four metrics you understand, in
  your own test suite, is a better first step than a platform you half-configure.
</Callout>

## A note on lock-in

Traces are the sticky part, not the metrics. Metric implementations are a few dozen lines
each and you can move them; a year of production traces in a hosted schema is a migration.
If that worries you, weigh the self-hostable options — Langfuse, Phoenix, Opik — before the
volume makes the decision for you.
